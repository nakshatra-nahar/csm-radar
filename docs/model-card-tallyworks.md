# Model card: tallyworks

Generated by `radar eval --tenant tallyworks` from the synthetic world. Each CSM works up to 10 accounts from their book each week.

## Summary

- In an average test week the model's list of up to 10 per CSM held 60% (95% CI 50% to 68%) of the ARR that went on to churn within 90 days.
- Sorting by renewal date held 18%, the rules heuristic 50% and random picks 20%.
- Renewal sort does no better than random because accounts near renewal have mostly already announced their decision and left the worklist, so the ones left are mostly renewers.
- Model minus renewal sort: +41 points (95% CI +31 to +49). Model minus rules: +10 points (95% CI +2 to +18).
- An oracle that sees true account health reaches 68%: the gap is what better data could still buy.
- All acceptance gates pass.

## How it was tested

Snapshots are Mondays. 4 origins, 8 weeks apart, cover the last 32 weeks with settled labels (2025-11-03 to 2026-06-08). At each origin the model is refit, as training deploys it (Platt step included), on snapshots whose labels had settled by then (at least 127 days older: the 90-day window, the 30-day reactivation check and a week of delivery lag), labelled only with what had arrived by the origin, then scored on the next 8 weeks. Test labels use full hindsight. A book is the accounts a CSM owns at the snapshot.

## Recall@K by policy

Recall (ARR-weighted) and precision are computed each week across every CSM's book, then averaged over test weeks. Lift is precision over the base rate. Every policy ranks the same rows by its expected ARR at risk (score x ARR). As served, a slot takes only an account whose key is at least min_slot_lift (1.0) x the tenant's mean key that week, so a low-risk book gets fewer than K; the floor only shortens a list, never reorders it. Random and the sorts have no floor. Here the mean key is over the labelled test rows, which leave out accounts whose decision is already visible (a shortcut: the served mean is over every eligible account).

## Ranking quality and calibration

Reliability of the model's probability, 10 quantile bins per segment. Training (design §6) adds a Platt step only if the error (ECE) exceeds 0.02 in a segment with at least 300 rows on the latest 8 weeks, and keeps it only if it lowers the worst such segment's error. The backtest scores each origin's model as training would deploy it: Platt was applied at 4 of 4 origins.

- billed monthly: 13,351 rows, ECE 0.017
- billed yearly: 6,146 rows, ECE 0.020

## Lead time

Of 174 churn decisions in the test weeks, the model flagged 65 (on its list for 2 weeks in a row) before the decision. Median lead: 11.2 weeks. 26% of all decisions were flagged at least 6 weeks ahead. The label horizon caps lead time at about 13 weeks.

## Confidence intervals

Paired cluster bootstrap over accounts, 1,000 resamples: an account's weeks stay together, and both policies are scored on the same resample. The differences are in the Summary.

## Leakage check: hindsight, point-in-time and naive current-state

| features | recall@K | ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|
| point-in-time (deployed) | 60% | 0.665 |
| hindsight: late data arrives on time | 59% | 0.666 |
| naive current-state: today's Salesforce row | 96% | 0.968 |

Hindsight features use every record as if it had arrived when it happened: +0.001 AUC, so late arrivals barely change this world's weekly snapshots.
The naive current-state model sees each Salesforce account row (Type, ARR__c, Renewal_Date__c) as it stands at history end in every snapshot. The registry computes the same features from it, plus former_customer, so a churned account is already a Former Customer; its +0.303 AUC is the optimism the as-of rule prevents.

## Acceptance gates

- Oracle ROC-AUC 0.843 (band 0.80 to 0.92).
- Best single feature: seat_utilization, ROC-AUC 0.695 (at most 0.72).
- Full observable model, in sample: ROC-AUC 0.715 (at most 0.86).
- Model recall@K beats renewal sort on the latest origin (2026-04-20): yes (59% against 18%).
- Coefficient signs: all match.

Result: all gates pass.

## Known limits

- Seat changes carry weak signal: seat_change_180d alone has ROC-AUC 0.53. usage_drop also moves when a customer cuts seats, so some seat-cut risk shows up as a usage drop.
- Capacity is a cap: a slot goes only to an account whose expected ARR at risk is at least the tenant mean, so a healthy book gets fewer than K. The backtest takes that mean over labelled test rows, not every eligible account.
- Gradient boosting is a diagnostic row: does the linear model miss structure? It has no serving path.
- Causes with few decisions have noisy recall. The world is synthetic: these numbers show the method works, not what a real book will do.
